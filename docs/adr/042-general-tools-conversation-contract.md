# ADR-042: General tools conversation contract

## Status

**Accepted — 2026-10-15, IR-589. Approved by Jive Tyler Revalde in the IR-589 review conversation.** This accepts the product and architecture contract below, including the owner's six recorded choices. It does not approve a provider, paid evaluation, institutional-data transfer, production budget, or deployment before their separate gates are satisfied. The earlier proposed amendments in ADR-034, ADR-035, ADR-036, ADR-038, ADR-040 and ADR-041 are not retroactively accepted.

## Decision

Ask IRIS will use one conversational controller with composable, read-only research tools as **the default reader experience when the implementation is released**. There is no reader choice, opt-in, or on/off feature switch between the controller and fixed answer lanes. Existing domain helpers, permissions, disclosure, citation provenance, admission and durable-run fences remain application-owned. The controller may discover a pair before comparing it and may use no tool for an eligible direct transformation; the application alone executes tools. `PLAN` chooses actions and `ANSWER` composes prose. There is no mandatory model-visible planning ceremony or untasked model accessor. New runs pin their protocol and controller version; in-flight legacy runs finish under their pinned version and old transcripts remain readable through a safe projection. Rollback is a normal code/deployment rollback, not a reader-facing lane toggle.

Institutional facts and paper findings require currently authorized evidence. A general explanation, including one requested within Paper Chat, may be direct when it makes no claim about that paper or the institutional corpus. Paper Chat still limits every search to its selected paper. The answer makes its general basis clear where a reader could confuse it with a paper finding. Questions about a particular paper, repository holdings, counts or research findings require evidence. Once a question requires evidence, `empty`, `withheld_all` and `none_relevant` cannot produce different public clues about withheld material; no failed search licenses an unsupported corpus assertion. The earlier ADR-035 direct-route union and proposed Jev route are not mandatory gates in the new controller. Any provider used by the controller still needs its own approval and data policy.

Answers should be high quality, detailed when the request warrants it, and responsive to the whole task. A tool error does not automatically discard useful work already supported by authorized evidence. The controller may try another permitted tool within the same budget, then answer the supported parts, state the material limitation plainly, and distinguish unavailable evidence from a finding that a paper did not report something. It must not claim that an incomplete search was exhaustive, invent a paper fact, expose withheld material, or present general knowledge as a finding from the corpus. If the requested task cannot be supported at all, it gives an honest unavailable or insufficient-evidence result rather than fabricated research prose.

Validation checks source identity/version, current access, citation bindings, numeric facts and completeness claims. One actionable repair is allowed within the same run budget. If repair fails, independently supported parts may be delivered with unsupported claims omitted or qualified; revoked or stale content is fenced. This **supersedes ADR-038's proposed whole-answer withholding rule for the new controller**, including its earlier recorded owner choice, without changing the legacy implementation. The final validated answer is delivered atomically; unvalidated factual tokens are not streamed to the reader.

The public protocol has separate `activity`, optional `reasoning_summary`, and `answer` fields. Activity contains neutral lifecycle states such as Waiting, Thinking and Preparing your answer. An optional, collapsed summary contains validated analytical observations tied to the answer and evidence. It is not a tool trace, citation, authorization decision or raw chain of thought. Tool names, arguments, routing labels and probabilities, provider identifiers, raw reasoning and diagnostic events are excluded by the server serializer on live, replayed and historical reader surfaces, including Paper Chat and export. An unavailable or unsafe summary is omitted. A reasoning-only provider reply is unavailable, not a successful answer. A new display summary has explicit provenance, follows its Turn's ownership/deletion lifecycle, and is never evidence. Stored old reasoning is hidden in the new reader projection but is not deleted by this decision; changing its retention needs a separate migration decision.

Place a persistent, accessible notice directly below the Ask IRIS and Paper Chat prompt boxes: **“Ask IRIS can make mistakes. Check important details against the cited papers.”** It remains visible before submission and is not substituted for citations, validation, access controls or honest uncertainty. The reader is responsible for checking the paper, while IRIS remains responsible for not knowingly presenting unsupported or inaccessible claims as paper findings.

Every controller, tool, retry, fallback, repair and optional summary attempt remains within the original deadline and shared admission budget. Cancellation fences new paid calls and state writes; a remote call already sent may still finish and be charged. The fake-test profile supplies no production limits. Optional public research remains a separate, disabled capability requiring its own data and security approvals; the absence of an on/off switch here concerns the **institutional conversational controller**, not optional public search.

## Evaluation and release gates

The deterministic [A01–A15 fixture pack](../../backend/apps/ai/evaluation/fixtures/general_tools_contract_v1.json) defines expected new behavior separately from current source-observed failure wording. Its validator checks fixture integrity; a database-backed control exercises the existing HTTP/SSE/Conversation path with fake providers but cannot prove the new controller works. [The evaluation protocol](../evaluation/general-tools/README.md) defines development and held-out conversations, human labels, manifest and proposed quality targets. The answer-quality objective includes detailed, cited responses, useful partial results after tool failures, and an honest account of what could not be checked.

Before the new controller replaces the reader path, the downstream work must implement the contract and pass database, security, public-serializer, accessibility and durable-worker checks. Named owners must approve provider/data policies and production cost/queue limits. Paid evaluation, institutional transmission and deployment each retain their separate approvals. IR-589 records the decision; it is not evidence that the implementation or rollout gates have passed.
