# 09 — Baseline Data

Everything here must come from RDCO and the reviewing offices. **No value has been invented, estimated or inferred.** Every field is blank.

**Collection window: Weeks 1–3. It does not reopen.** Once IRIS is in use, the manual process stops being observable and recall becomes contaminated by exposure to the new system.

---

## Why this matters

Three separate things depend on it:

1. **The problem statement.** *"Redundant office review is a real cost"* is currently an assumption. M18 is what makes it a finding.
2. **The effort approximation.** Converting "reviews preserved" into "hours preserved" needs a real figure for what a repeat review costs.
3. **Evaluation design.** Submission volume and decline rate determine whether Phase 3 can use organic data or must rely entirely on scenario sessions ([07](07-workflow-evaluation.md)).

Without it the thesis can still evaluate the contribution — Strand B is self-contained — but it cannot say the problem was worth solving.

## Current pain points — open questions

Qualitative, gathered in the SME interview ([06](06-validation-instruments.md) A3, questions 1–7). Not a form to fill.

- Where does time go that the offices consider wasted?
- What do they currently do that the system would have to replace, not just digitise?
- What information do they lack when a submission arrives?
- What goes wrong most often?

---

## Recording conventions

To keep the baseline defensible:

- **Record the source of every figure** — extracted from records, or staff estimate. They carry different evidential weight and must be reported differently.
- **Record confidence** — High / Medium / Low, as judged by the respondent.
- **Record ranges, not false precision.** "Two to four weeks" is more honest than "21 days" if that is what was said.
- **Attribute per office** where figures differ. A single institutional average may hide the variation that matters.
- **Where RDCO cannot answer, record that explicitly.** "Not available" is data. Inventing a plausible figure is not.

## If the data cannot be obtained

Consequences, so the fallback is planned rather than improvised:

| Missing | Consequence | Fallback |
|---|---|---|
| Volume and decline rate (1–9) | Cannot size the organic strand | Assume it is insufficient; **Strand B becomes the sole evaluation** |
| Turnaround (10–15) | No efficiency baseline for context | Report IRIS timings without comparison; state the limitation |
| **Redundant review (16–21)** | **No effort approximation** | Estimate M6 from Phase 3's own repeat-vs-first review timings. Weaker — it measures IRIS, not current practice — but it is not nothing |
| Office structure (22–25) | Cannot plan sessions | Escalate immediately; this blocks Phase 3 scheduling |
| Historical records (26–27) | Baseline rests on recall alone | Acceptable; report as recall-based |
| **Pilot commitment (29)** | **No organic strand** | Strand B only; report the absence of ecological data as a limitation |

**None of these fallbacks involves inventing a number.** Where data is absent, the thesis says so.
