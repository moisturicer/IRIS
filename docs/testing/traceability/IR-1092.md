# IR-1092: Phone record bar and 44 px touch targets

**Requirements:** the audit findings RES-03 (on a phone the record chrome takes a fifth of the screen) and RES-02 (many touch targets are 34–38 px), in `docs/frontend_ui_audit_spec.md` §4.7 (IR-1071). The matrix has no `FR-` row for them. Epic IR-920.

## What was wrong

- Below 640 px the record bar wrapped onto two rows: Back and the title on the first (56 px, the title's own height), a full-width Overview / Paper / Review control on the second, so the chrome was about 170 px under the app's 56 px top bar.
- Measured by the audit at 360 px: the bell was 34 px, Close menu 36 px, sidebar links 38 px. The shared `Button` sizes `sm` (`py-1.5`) and `md` (`py-2`) have no touch size at all, so every control built on them was about 32–37 px.

## What changed

**Client only; no server change.**

- **`ReadingHeader.tsx`: one 56 px row at every width** (`h-14 flex-nowrap`). Below `reading-split` (640 px of the `reading` container) Back, and Back to answer when a citation adds it, are 44 px icon buttons whose labels stay as `sr-only` text; the section control takes the rest of the row; the title stays as the page's `h1` for screen readers but is not drawn. From `reading-split` nothing moves. `READING_HEADER_PX` was already 56, so the sticky offsets under the bar are now true on a phone.
- **The version leaves the bar and goes to the reader's toolbar** below 640 px (`PaperPdfReader`'s new `toolbarStart`). `PaperViewPage` reads the measured tier and hands the picker, or a single version's label, to exactly one of the two places, so it is drawn once and stays in the accessibility tree. Replaces the earlier plan of hiding it, which removed it from the tree and left a reviewer no way to switch version from the Paper or Review tab.
- **A `touch:` variant** (`tailwind.config.js`: `(pointer: coarse)`, defined after the window breakpoints so it wins over a `md:` compact size). The shared `Button` sizes `sm`, `md` and `lg` get `touch:min-h-11`, and `icon` `touch:w-11 touch:h-11`. This is a pointer test, not a width: a touch tablet gets 44 px at any window width, a mouse never does, and no window breakpoint is used to size a record-page control.
- **`components/layout`:** the bell 44 px below `md` and on touch (34 px otherwise), Close menu 44 px, navigation links, Help and the account menu's items `min-h-11` below `md` and on touch, the bell's Mark all read and View all notifications likewise, the rail's IRIS mark 44 px on touch. The tablet rail's `md:h-10` links are 44 px on touch because `min-h-11` outranks the fixed height.
- **`features/records/paper-view`:** Files' Download / Remove, Review's All documents, the IP-type chips and Save classification, the revision-upload label, Manage documents (measured at 141 × 18 px), the mention and severity choices and the review-thread filter are 44 px on touch. From `reading-split` Download / Remove, All documents, Manage documents, the mention and severity choices and the thread filter keep a 36 px size; the IP-type chips, Save classification and the upload label were about 28–32 px (`py-1` / `py-1.5`) and are now 36 px, and the chips went from `px-2.5` to `px-3`.
- `DESIGN.md` states the phone record bar, where the version goes and the touch rule.

## Evidence

Local, 2026-11-14, Windows. `tsc --noEmit` clean. The new and changed unit tests were run failing first, then passing, and no existing test was changed to pass (the layout tests written earlier on this branch were extended to expect the `touch:` classes).

**Browser measurement.** `npm run build`, then a temporary Playwright spec (deleted, not committed) on the installed Chrome with `hasTouch` and `isMobile`, against the smoke tier's mocked API (`e2e/`). For each state it recorded the record bar's height, every visible interactive element under 43.5 px on either side, and the document's `scrollWidth` against `clientWidth`. States: Overview, the Review workspace (a participant opening `?section=paper`), the real Paper tab (a non-participant reading a manuscript), the open phone drawer and the bell's menu, each at 390 and 360 px. Reader toolbar, tab bar, corner buttons and version picker were among the 11–16 controls counted per page. Screenshots at 390 and 360 px showed the one-row bar, the version picker at the start of the toolbar and the end-of-page clearance under the paper.

**Not measured:** dialogs and menus other than the bell's (the review dialogs get their 44 px from the `Button` classes, asserted but not measured); the comment, saved-reply and Files states, which need data the smoke fixtures do not have; Safari and a real handset (the emulation is Chrome's); the tablet widths; a hybrid device whose primary pointer is a mouse (`pointer: coarse` is false there, so it gets the compact sizes).

## Limits and decisions for the reviewer

- **`reading-split` is a container query, not a phone query.** A narrow reading container at a wide window, such as a tablet with the sidebar open, also gets the one-row bar with an icon-only Back and no drawn title. That is the same rule the other workspace tiers follow (DESIGN.md: "from the `reading` width"), so it is kept as intended rather than changed.
- **The reading surfaces keep their container-based scale** (44 px below 640 px of the `reading` width, 36 px from it, `controls.ts`). A touch tablet whose workspace is 640 px or wider gets 36 px on those controls, while the shared `Button` and the app shell use the pointer test. Moving the reading scale to `touch:` is a wider change to a standing DESIGN.md rule and was not made here.
- **The `touch:` variant changes every `Button` on a touch device** at any width (Discover, Publish, dialogs), not only on record pages. That is the audit's "one control height per input mode", but it is a visible change on tablets.
- The section control has about 232–236 px for three tabs at 360 px beside Back and Back to answer; the tabs may truncate. It was not measured with a citation's Back to answer present.
