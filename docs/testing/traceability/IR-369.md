# IR-369: a paper's views count each account once; Trending is views gained in the last 30 days

**Requirements:** no FR- id. The rule was decided by Hermoine Zozobrado on 2026-10-31 (it replaces the 2026-09-25 rule of once per reader per paper per 24 hours) and is defined in `CONTEXT.md` (**View**, **Trending**). ADR-027 carries a note on what it means for access velocity (IR-304).

## What changed

- **`RecordView`** (`records/0039`): one row per account per paper, with when it was first opened. A unique constraint on (paper, account) is what makes one account count once, even for two simultaneous openings. It cascades with the paper; account deletion deletes the person's rows (`accounts/deletion/registry.py`).
- **`increment_access`** now calls `apps/records/views_count.py::record_opening`:
  - It counts a view only for a published paper and an account that isn't one of its owners. The adviser, reviewers and office staff count.
  - It raises `access_count` in the database (`F() + 1`), not as "read, add one, write".
  - It writes the `ACCESS` audit event for every opening, counted or not, at most once per account per paper per rolling 24 hours. A transaction-scoped advisory lock per (paper, account) stops two tabs opened at once from both writing one.
  - It still answers `{"access_count": n}`, so Paper View is unchanged. `get_object()` still applies `visible_to`, so a paper the caller can't see is still the missing-record 404 (IR-153).
- **Trending:** `GET /records/?ordering=trending` (`records/filters.py::RecordOrderingFilter`) orders by views gained in the last 30 days, then all-time views, then newest. Discover's Trending feed asks for it (`discoverQuery.ts`); Most Viewed stays `-access_count`.
- **Rebuild** (`records/0040`): replaces every count from `ACCESS` history. One view per distinct live, non-owner account per published paper, dated by that account's first opening after the paper's first `PUBLISHED` audit event; a paper with no such event (published before IR-144) counts every opening. A paper that isn't published gets 0. Reversing deletes the rows and keeps the counts.
- **`seed_demo`** no longer invents view counts: they would claim readers that don't exist.
- **Docs:** ADR-027 note; `docs/compliance/IR-123-processing-review.md` moves view counting from *planned* into the code inventory. `CONTEXT.md` already defined **View** and **Trending** as built.

## Decisions made while building (for review)

1. **A deleted account's views stay in the count.** Its rows go, as the rule says, but `access_count` isn't lowered: the view happened, and the count names no one. Trending's 30-day figure, which counts rows, does lose them.
2. **A soft-deleted paper keeps its rows.** Soft deletion is reversible (a rejected delete request restores the paper), so the rows go only when the paper itself is removed from the database.
3. **Openings that don't count are still audited**, once a day: an owner reading their own paper, or an office reading one in review. The audit log records access; it isn't the view count.
4. **The published data notice isn't changed.** It is a versioned record the System Admin publishes (ADR-054), and its wording waits on IR-123's DPO review. The inventory says so.
5. **A saved feed made from Trending still stores "Most viewed"** (`feedQueryOf`), which was the same ordering until now. Saved feeds have no Trending sort; left for that feature's owner to decide.

## Evidence

Local, 2026-11-12, Windows, a throwaway `pgvector` Postgres container:

- `apps/records/test_record_views.py`, **22 tests**, all through the API except the rebuild's, which call the migration's function on crafted audit history:
  - one account once, a second account once more; owners and co-owners never; an unpublished paper never, for its adviser or RDCO; the adviser and office staff on a published paper;
  - an unseen paper's 404 is byte-identical to a missing id's; no response names who viewed;
  - `ACCESS` events, read through the System Admin's audit log: one per day of reloads, another after 24 hours, a rolling window across midnight, uncounted openings audited once;
  - simultaneous openings, held on the paper's row until both are waiting (the IR-644 pattern): two accounts both count; one account counts once and is audited once;
  - Trending and Most Viewed order;
  - the rebuild: owners, deleted accounts, events without an account and openings before publication left out; first opening after publication as the date; no publication date; an unpublished paper at 0; no second count after the rebuild.
- `apps/accounts/test_account_deletion.py`: **23 passed**, including the new case that a deleted account's view rows go and the count stays, and the registry guard.
- Each rule was checked to be load-bearing by breaking it and watching its tests fail: the published and owner checks; the 24-hour window and the audit check; the atomic increment (a read-add-write with a widened window gave `1 != 2`); the unique constraint (removed from the migration: `2 != 1` twice); the advisory lock (the same-account audit test failed `2 != 1` before it existed); and the rebuild's owner and publication-date filters.
- Frontend: `src/features/discover` **123 passed**; `tsc --noEmit` clean; eslint has the same 2 warnings as `main`.
- `flake8` clean on the touched backend files; `makemigrations --check` reports no changes.
