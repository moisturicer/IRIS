# IR-369: a paper's views count each account once; Trending is views gained in the last 30 days

**Requirements:** no FR- id. The rule was decided by Hermoine Zozobrado on 2026-10-31 (it replaces the 2026-09-25 rule of once per reader per paper per 24 hours) and is defined in `CONTEXT.md` (**View**, **Trending**). ADR-027 carries a note on what it means for access velocity (IR-304).

## What changed

- **`RecordView`** (`records/0039`): one row per account per paper, with when it was first opened. A unique constraint on (paper, account) is what makes one account count once, even for two simultaneous openings. It goes with the paper: the cascade, and `soft_delete_record`, which is the final deletion an approved delete request makes. Account deletion deletes the person's rows (`accounts/deletion/registry.py`). The personal-data download lists them as `papers_viewed`: each paper, its title while the person may still read it, and when they first opened it (`accounts/personal_data/`).
- **`increment_access`** now calls `apps/records/views_count.py::record_opening`:
  - It counts a view only for a published paper and an account that isn't one of its owners. The adviser, reviewers and office staff count.
  - It raises `access_count` in the database (`F() + 1`), not as "read, add one, write".
  - It writes the `ACCESS` audit event for every opening, counted or not, at most once per account per paper per rolling 24 hours. A transaction-scoped advisory lock per (paper, account) stops two tabs opened at once from both writing one.
  - It still answers `{"access_count": n}`, so Paper View is unchanged. `get_object()` still applies `visible_to`, so a paper the caller can't see is still the missing-record 404 (IR-153).
- **Trending:** `GET /records/?ordering=trending` (`records/filters.py::RecordOrderingFilter`) orders by views gained in the last 30 days, then all-time views, then newest. Discover's Trending feed asks for it (`discoverQuery.ts`); Most Viewed stays `-access_count`.
- **Rebuild** (`records/0040`): replaces every count from `ACCESS` history.
  - One view per distinct non-owner account per published paper, dated by that account's first opening after the paper's first `PUBLISHED` audit event. A paper with no such event (published before IR-144) counts every opening.
  - An account deleted since still counts, but gets no row (decision 1).
  - A published paper awaiting its delete review counts as published, since declining restores it. Any other paper gets 0, including one whose deletion was approved.
  - Reversing deletes the rows and keeps the counts.
- **`seed_demo`** no longer invents view counts: they would claim readers that don't exist.
- **Docs:** ADR-027 note; `docs/compliance/IR-123-processing-review.md` moves view counting from *planned* into the code inventory. `CONTEXT.md` already defined **View** and **Trending** as built.

## Decisions made while building (for review)

1. **A deleted account's views stay in the count** (decided by Hermoine Zozobrado, 2026-11-12, after review).
   - Its rows go, as the rule says, but `access_count` isn't lowered, live or in the rebuild.
   - Cost: Trending's 30-day figure counts rows, so it loses them, and all-time views can exceed the rows.
2. **Deleting a paper means approving its delete request.** `soft_delete_record` is the final deletion and removes the rows. Only a paper still awaiting review (`pending_delete`, not deleted) can be restored, and it keeps them. The first version of this branch kept rows on soft deletion; the spec review caught it.
3. **Openings that don't count are still audited**, once a day: an owner reading their own paper, or an office reading one in review. The audit log records access; it isn't the view count.
4. **The published data notice isn't changed.** It is a versioned record the System Admin publishes (ADR-054), and its wording waits on IR-123's DPO review. The inventory says so.
5. **A saved feed made from Trending still stores "Most viewed"** (`feedQueryOf`), which was the same ordering until now. Saved feeds have no Trending sort; left for that feature's owner to decide.
6. **Owners are judged as of now.** The rebuild leaves out today's owners, so a former owner's openings count. Live, an account that viewed and later became an owner keeps its view.
