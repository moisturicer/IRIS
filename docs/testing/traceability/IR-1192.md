## IR-1192 — Thread replies say who wrote them

**Basis and scope.** IR-1192 under epic IR-920; authority ADR-032 §7 (a thread belongs to its writer's party). Built on IR-1191's row layout, on the same branch at the owner's direction.

**What changed, backend.**
- `apps/reviews/comments.py`: a reply now stores its writer's party when it is posted (`writer_party`, the rule a thread uses). The payload gives a reply `party_label`: the party in the server's words ("ITSO", "Adviser"...), **"Author"** (`threads.AUTHOR_LABEL`) for one of the record's owners, and null where neither holds.
- **Departs from the AC's example wording "The authors", at the owner's direction (screenshot, 2026-11-16):** a reply names a person, so it reads "Author"; a thread still reads "The authors".
- **Known limit:** "Author" is decided when the payload is read, from the record's current owners (an owner's stored party is blank, as on a thread). A reply by someone later removed as an owner shows no label. A reviewer's party is stored, so it is the one held when writing. `0030` places old replies by `0025`'s rule (the Seat held by the time of writing), which can differ from `writer_party` for a reviewer holding two Seats at once. `party` stays null on a reply: it is not a thread. Every thread rule already excludes replies (`reply_to__isnull=True`), so storing a reply's party changes no count, block or permission.
- `apps/reviews/migrations/0030_backfill_reply_parties.py`: existing replies get the party `0025` would have given them: blank for an owner, else the writer's Seat on that record (the latest opened by the time they wrote, or failing that any). Only `party` is written.
- No new access: a reply and its label go out only through `may_read_review`, as before.

**What changed, frontend.** `ReviewThreads.tsx`: a reply row has an avatar, the writer's name, the label (none for an older payload), the date and "edited"; mentions still show. The Reply control shows the count, named "Reply, N replies". Where the server says a reply is the viewer's (`can_edit`) and they may comment, a ⋯ menu at the row's far right offers **Edit**, which swaps the text for a form in place and saves through `PATCH /records/<id>/review-comments/<id>/` (mentions sent only when changed). `RowMenu` gained a `compact` trigger for this.

**Tests.**
- Backend, `test_review_threads.py::AReplyNamesItsWriterTests`: the author's, an office reviewer's (ITSO) and the Adviser's replies carry "Author", "ITSO", "Adviser", on post and on the list; the label survives the writer's part being done; a stranger gets a 404 with no reply and no label. `test_review_threads_migration.py`: `0030` gives owner, Adviser, ITSO and removed-account replies their parties and rewrites nothing else.
- Frontend, `ReviewThreads.test.tsx`, "a thread's replies": name, label, date and "edited", a reply with no label, the counted Reply control, editing one's own reply from its menu, and no menu without `comment_review` or on someone else's reply.

**Runs.** Frontend: as IR-1191's note. Backend: **not run locally** (this worktree has no `backend/.env` or Postgres); the new tests and the migration test run on CI. The migration has not been run against a copy of a realistic database.
