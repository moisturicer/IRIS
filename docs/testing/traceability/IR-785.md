## IR-785 — A person requests account deletion, and the System Admin confirms it once nobody is waiting on them

**Basis and scope.** IR-785 under IR-779; authority ADR-051 decisions 8 to 12 and 28. The page is IR-788. RDCO's acts that clear blockers are IR-797 (open, not merged); until then only a coordinator's reassign and RDCO's existing acts clear them. No FR- id.

**API** (`apps/accounts/views.py`, `apps/accounts/deletion/`):

| Route | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| `GET /users/me/deletion-request/` | any signed-in person | their latest request, or `null` |
| `POST /users/me/deletion-request/` | any signed-in person | asks; 409 when one is already pending |
| `DELETE /users/me/deletion-request/` | any signed-in person | cancels the pending one; 409 when none is |
| `GET /users/deletion-requests/` | the System Admin | pending requests, each with `blockers` and `capabilities` |
| `POST /users/deletion-requests/<id>/confirm/` | the System Admin, not on their own | 409 with `blockers` while any remain; 409 when no longer pending |
| `POST /users/deletion-requests/<id>/reject/` `{reason}` | the System Admin, not on their own | 400 without a reason |

RDCO gets 403 on all three System Admin routes. `AccountDeletionRequest` (migration `accounts/0019`) allows at most one pending request per person, enforced by a partial unique constraint. It reuses `RequestStatus`: `approved` is a confirmed deletion and `declined` a rejection.

**Blockers** (`deletion/blockers.py`), checked again inside the confirming transaction:
- a paper in review with no other owner: "RDCO must withdraw it or add an owner";
- an open seat on a paper in review (ADR-032 §4), or an active Adviser assignment on a paper they advise: "a coordinator must reassign it" or "RDCO must replace the Adviser".

**On confirmation, in one transaction** (`deletion/service.py`):
- The person's sole-owner drafts are soft-deleted. Papers with another owner carry on.
- If they were the Contact person (`RecordOwner.is_primary`, which decides who gets review emails), the remaining owner with the earliest ownership row takes over and is told in-app.
- On a published paper they advised, the name is copied to the new `Record.adviser_name` and `adviser` is cleared, so the name links to nobody (`records/0034`). The record serializers read the stored name.
- Every relation to `User` is kept, deleted or anonymized, as `deletion/registry.py` declares with a reason for each row.
  - **Deleted:** profiles, pending role and download requests, recovery tokens and intents, discovery data, notifications and read marks, Ask IRIS conversations, queued questions, research runs, screening verdicts and saved papers.
  - **Kept:** reviews, comments, seats, routing, files, audit rows, a Call's Posted by, and ownership rows.
- The `User` row is kept and changed:
  - the name becomes "Former user";
  - the email becomes `deleted-<id>@deleted.invalid`, so the real address can register again;
  - no role and no coordinator flag;
  - inactive, with an unusable password and a bumped `credential_version`;
  - every session is ended (new `SessionEndReason.DELETED`) and every token is blacklisted;
  - `deleted_at` is stamped.
- The outcome email goes after commit, to the address they had.

A deleted account disappears from:
- RDCO's Users page and the user detail (404);
- the locked list;
- the Adviser picker and reviewer pools, which already filter on `is_active`.

**AI runtime.** Its Conversations live in `iris_ai`, which Django doesn't write. `ai/0043` makes `iris_authz.reader_status()` read a stamped account as `gone`, the same as a missing row, so `python -m ai.runs.purge` deletes those Conversations. Before this change it would have read `inactive`, which only fences them. The change narrows access and admits nobody new.

**Audit:** `ACCOUNT_DELETION_REQUESTED`, `_CANCELLED`, `_CONFIRMED` and `_REJECTED` (`audit/0017`). The confirmation row names the old address, because audit rows are never changed (ADR-051 consequences).

**Choices made while building, for review:**
- "Longest-standing remaining owner" is the earliest `RecordOwner` row, because ownership has no date.
- The System Admin sees the blocking paper's title, so the plain-words reason can name the paper. It has no other read access to papers in review.
- A System Admin may ask to delete their own account. Another System Admin decides it.
- Decided role requests and a person's paper-deletion requests are kept as history. The criteria name only pending role and download requests.

**Tests:**
- `backend/apps/accounts/test_account_deletion.py`, 21 tests:
  - The registry names every `User` relation and nothing else, so a new model fails until it's declared.
  - Requesting: a person asks, holds at most one pending request, and can cancel it.
  - Deciding: only the System Admin decides, never on their own request. A rejection needs a reason, which reaches the person in-app and by email. A stale decision gets 409.
  - Blockers: each one refuses confirmation, and the list shows it with `can_confirm` false. A co-owned paper and a draft don't block.
  - Confirmation erases the person's details and private data.
  - The row, ownership, reviews and audit rows survive.
  - A published paper's Adviser name stays as text, and the Contact person passes on.
  - Sign-in is refused, sessions end and tokens are blacklisted.
  - The account is gone from the Users page and the Adviser picker, and the freed email can register again.
- `apps/tests/access/table.py` declares the four routes, and `test_authorization_matrix.py` adds the System Admin-only case.
